\begin{abstract}
Class imbalance is usually treated as the main obstacle to predicting rare clinical outcomes, yet adding minority-class records cannot repair a decision boundary that is intrinsically weak. We ask whether class separability, rather than class frequency alone, should be an explicit target of synthetic tabular health-data generation. Preliminary experiments with resampling, unconstrained conditional-GAN (CTGAN) augmentation, and domain-anchored causal-plausibility filtering produced only limited or inconsistent predictive gains, which motivated a neighbourhood and explainability analysis that implicated class overlap, rather than imbalance per se, as the more plausible bottleneck. Building on this diagnosis, we developed \sepa{}, a post-generation selection method that oversamples a CTGAN candidate pool and returns a class-count-preserving synthetic training set chosen by weighting realism, label separability, and domain-anchor compatibility. All predictive estimates use leakage-controlled five-fold stratified cross-validation, training on synthetic data and evaluating on held-out real folds, applied to two imbalanced health datasets: a 5,000-record sample of the Brazilian Vigitel obesity survey and a 334-record COVID-19 hospital-mortality cohort. A blocked $2^2$ factorial experiment shows that separability pressure accounts for 96.0\% of treatment-plus-error variation in Vigitel synthetic-to-real Macro-F1 ($p=5.03\times10^{-10}$), while anchor pressure and the interaction term are negligible; the same design explains only 5.1\% of variation in COVID-19 mortality, where residual error dominates (81.5\%) and no factor reaches significance. Explicit separability control therefore appears able to recover useful class-conditional structure in some settings, but the effect is dataset-dependent and is equally consistent with selection of easier prototypes rather than genuine signal recovery. We discuss this rival explanation, the statistical limits of cross-validation-based replication, and the additional generator baselines, minority-diversity and privacy analyses, and external validation still required before the method's benefit can be generalised. The contribution offered here is not a universally superior generator, but transparent, auditable evidence that class-boundary preservation is a distinct and controllable, though dataset-dependent, property of synthetic clinical data.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item \sepa{} selects synthetic tabular records by realism, separability, and domain anchors.
\item Class rebalancing and unconstrained CTGAN augmentation gave inconsistent utility gains.
\item A blocked factorial design attributes the Vigitel utility gain to separability, not anchors.
\item Separability explains 96\% of Vigitel variation but only 5\% in COVID-19 mortality data.
\item The effect is dataset-dependent; broader baselines and privacy testing are still required.
\end{highlights}
